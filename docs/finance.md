# Freight finance

Freight audit now contains four workspaces: Carrier audit, Customer billing, Bank reconciliation, and Journal. All amounts are integer minor units in their shipment's [currency](multi-currency.md) (USD by default). Finance/admin credentials can change these records; other tenant staff can read them and download their tenant's statements and journal exports. The separate [load-board CSV](load-exports.md) preserves signed commercial amounts as numeric decimals, including negative margins and credits; it does not post accounting entries.

Staff workspace responses build one borrowed financial index per immutable snapshot. Cash allocations, credits, approved invoices, adjustments, cancellation obligations, recognition history and activity dates are selected without repeatedly scanning the complete tenant history for every bill and settlement. Original order and first-match rules remain intact; the index is discarded before returning the response and never becomes write authority. Display arithmetic refuses unrepresentable signed credits, refunds and account balances with a checked error.

## Customer billing

Configure Billing identity with your issuing legal name, address, billing contact, and payment instructions. The [reviewed identity editor](billing-identity-review.md) preserves the version and verified tenant/operator that opened the form, so a background refresh cannot silently overwrite another operator’s legal or payment details. A real workspace starts without these details. Synthetic demo identity is explicitly labeled. Add the customer's bill-to address in its master record or supply the address on the invoice draft.

Create a draft for a delivered load. The amount comes from the agreed inclusive base sell rate plus separately approved customer additions, never a browser-supplied total. One non-void original customer invoice is allowed per load. Numbers are normalized to uppercase, remain unique, and cannot be reused after voiding. Drafts can change number, due date, and bill-to address with an expected version. Approval/voiding of a customer addition refreshes the original draft basis and increments its version; pending customer proposals hold issuance. [Charge proposals and agreement evidence](charges.md).

Issuance requires delivered status, accepted POD evidence, a billing issuer, and a bill-to address. Stored POD bytes are checked inside the same database transaction. The invoice freezes its customer, issuer/payment instructions, bill-to address, shipment, source load version, currency, and sell amount. Later master-data/profile changes do not alter it. Its posting debits accounts receivable and credits freight revenue. Issuance does not send a message or establish customer receipt.

Download an HTML invoice to inspect, print, save as PDF, or pass to your billing process. It includes the agreed sell amount, credits, reconciled receipts/refunds, and outstanding balance; carrier buy cost, margin, internal review notes, and other customers' information are excluded. Draft/void documents are marked. Dynamic text is escaped, and downloads use attachment/no-store/nosniff protections.

Credit notes reduce the issued invoice amount with an explicit number, reason, and actual issue date. They debit freight revenue and credit accounts receivable. Total active credits cannot exceed the original amount, including after payment. A credit reducing received funds requires actual customer confirmation/evidence; the resulting negative receivable is a customer refund liability. Record an actual external return separately using bank direction `customer_refund`. Reverse an erroneous credit with an appended opposite posting; current invoice versions are required after refund history, and actual receipts/refunds remain. Ordinary invoice voiding requires zero net funds retained and no active credits. Voiding an issued invoice reverses its issuance journal; a void draft has no journal posting. [Paid-customer credit/refund workflow and correction rules](customer-refunds.md).

The UI shows original received, credited, refunded, and outstanding amounts separately, plus days past due for positive debt. Paid status means the invoice's net amount is covered by recorded net external receipts and credits. Fully credited invoices with no net receipts are labeled `credited`. Negative balances have status `credit_due`, except retracted invoices retain `void`. The summary separates positive receivables from `customer_credit_cents`; `net_receivable_cents` is signed. The platform records staff's external bank evidence; it does not independently verify a bank balance.

After original issuance, use **Draft supplement** on that original invoice to bill an additional amount supported by an actual customer agreement. Each supplement has its own number, due date, agreement/contact/confirmation, optional immutable supporting file, issuance, credits, and bank allocations. Its customer and shipment come from the frozen original; it does not repeat the original total. Issuance checks delivery and stored agreement bytes, posts separate receivable/revenue, and retains the original snapshot and payment history. Duplicate agreement references and implicit/branching corrections fail. Active supplements protect their original invoice from voiding. Load totals include issued non-void supplements and subtract active customer credit notes. [Supplement workflow, correction rules, and preview](customer-supplements.md).

## Carrier liabilities

Real workspaces require two named finance subjects for primary carrier invoice approval. **Review entry** records the actual carrier invoice reference and substantive findings against the displayed revision; **Approve** requires a different named finance subject and independent findings. Anyone who entered, corrected, classified a canonical revision, or reviewed that invoice's entry is excluded from approving it, including administrators and rotated credentials with the same subject. Legacy credential fingerprints and automated/system identities cannot perform these reviews. Configure stable scoped subjects or use staff sign-in, with at least two authorized people.

The review revision binds the invoice, current load version/assignment, retained entry/correction event identities, and the attached POD's current metadata and reviews. A stored-file review invalidates it even when the load version does not advance. Corrections back to the same amount still require a fresh entry review. Workspace and deferred-history responses include per-invoice `approval_control` with the revision, review subject, state, and allowed next action. The open review form retains its original revision. Server checks occur inside the same tenant transaction as posting and the receipt. Old approved records remain historical decisions; exact original receipts still replay without reinterpretation. Legacy unapproved invoices need an explicit current entry review; that attestation does not invent a historical creator or independently authenticate the carrier's document.

For an approved carrier invoice, **View approval** shows the recorded entry reviewer and independent approver, their original times with the viewer’s timezone, the carrier invoice evidence reference, and both sets of findings. It follows the exact `entry_review_id` and revision retained with the decision; approval itself changes the current invoice revision, and later POD/load updates do not erase or replace the original decision. The view is read-only and closes with Escape while returning focus to its link. By default, full and history-deferred API responses expose the same complete `approval_evidence`; the current action revision remains separate.

Approving a matched carrier invoice recognizes freight expense and carrier payable. Preparing its settlement does not post the same liability twice. Existing approved invoices from older snapshots are recognized once when their first settlement is prepared; this is an opening payable at the preparation date, not a reconstruction of historical books.

Settlements keep the matched approved invoice amount (original buy plus approved carrier additions) and a version. Their status moves between `prepared`, `partially_reconciled`, and `reconciled` as external payouts are recorded or reversed. Carrier invoices retain their approved state; a bank record is a separate entity. Pending carrier proposals hold invoice approval. Additional agreement approval re-audits an unposted invoice without altering its received amount. No transfer is initiated by approval, preparation, or reconciliation.

After primary approval, [carrier payable adjustments](payables.md) record actual supplemental invoices, replacement totals, and credit memos as separate drafts/postings. The original invoice and settlement principal stay fixed; effective settlement totals and versions include active posted differences. Credits after payment can create a signed negative balance (`credit_due`), shown separately as a carrier credit balance. Record an actual external refund using bank direction `carrier_refund`; cash debits and carrier payable credits settle that balance. Reversing an adjustment or refund appends its opposite and preserves prior approvals/bank history. Additional signed 210s remain unposted in finance classification until a named decision.

## Bank reconciliation

**Carrier credit applications** in this workspace record actual carrier-confirmed allocations from an existing credit-bearing settlement to 1–100 other prepared invoices for that carrier. They post carrier payable between loads, preserve invoice costs and bank history, and require current balances/versions plus named confirmation. Reversal/correction retains original proof and can reopen already-paid targets. Application files and every affected load's supporting evidence are checked transactionally. Current positive payables and negative credits stay separately visible until an actual recorded allocation/refund/payment resolves them. [Complete workflow and limits](carrier-credit-applications.md).

Record the actual external account label, bank reference, cleared date, bank amount, and reconciliation note. Allocate the amount to 1–100 customer invoices or prepared settlements. Every allocation is positive and uses the target's current version. Its total must equal the cleared bank amount exactly. A batch belongs to one customer for receipts/customer refunds or one carrier for payouts/carrier refunds. Customer refunds require actual confirmation and can consume only existing refund liabilities, including those on void cancellation invoices. Overpayments, stale allocations, duplicate targets, future clearing dates, and mixed counterparties are rejected atomically.

An account label plus bank reference can have only one active reconciliation, across all four directions. Idempotency replay returns the original result and does not post again. Distinct concurrent keys cannot bypass the bank-reference or outstanding-balance guards.

To correct an entry, reverse it with a reason and date. Reversal appends the opposite journal and reopens its balances; it does not reverse a bank transfer. The original entry, allocations, actor, and posting remain. A corrected record must explicitly identify the uncorrected reversed entry in `corrects_id` and keep that exact account, bank reference, and direction. It may correct amounts or allocation/counterparty mistakes. The old/new records link both ways; a correction chain cannot branch or reuse an active bank line. Each new batch must still have one counterparty and fit its current balances.

## Journals and integrity

| Action                               | Debit                           | Credit                         |
| ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Customer invoice issuance            | Accounts receivable             | Freight revenue                |
| Customer credit                      | Freight revenue                 | Accounts receivable            |
| Carrier invoice approval             | Freight expense                 | Carrier payable                |
| Reconciled customer receipt          | Cash                            | Accounts receivable            |
| Reconciled customer refund           | Accounts receivable             | Cash                           |
| Reconciled carrier payout            | Carrier payable                 | Cash                           |
| Supplemental carrier debit           | Freight expense                 | Carrier payable                |
| Carrier credit/reducing revision     | Carrier payable                 | Freight expense                |
| Reconciled carrier refund            | Cash                            | Carrier payable                |
| Confirmed carrier credit application | Carrier payable on target loads | Carrier payable on source load |
| Reversal                             | Original credits                | Original debits                |

Every posting is balanced, positive on exactly one side per line, tied to its source record, and attributed to the authenticated subject. Multi-load bank entries retain each line's load ID. Reversals reference the original journal ID. Business state, journal, audit, and idempotency receipts commit together under the tenant row lock. A failed mutation retains none of its candidate postings.

The Journal workspace summarizes debit, credit, and net debit amounts by account. Cash is movement recorded by Freight, not the organization's complete bank balance. CSV exports preserve source/reversal/load IDs and escape spreadsheet formulas in text. Customer and carrier obligations shown outside the journal derive from the same active allocations. Back up the full workspace aggregate and document table together.

Qualification includes a known scenario: customer invoice 149,000 cents, credit 9,000, receipts 140,000, carrier cost/payout 118,000. The subledger closes with zero receivable/payable and 22,000 cents of cash movement. Reversals reopen the original balances without modifying old journal lines. PostgreSQL tests use independent instances and reconnect to verify concurrency, persistence, and absence of failed-request residue.

## Existing StateSet stack and limits

The implementation follows the exact-money, invoice-state, payment-idempotency, and reversing-journal patterns in `stateset-icommerce` revision `767f23f3b32d10c8744c8e4642d875382c811f40`, inspected in its invoice models, PostgreSQL invoice repository, and embedded general-ledger code. It stays inside Freight's PostgreSQL transaction rather than creating a second commerce database with an uncoordinated write.

This is the freight operational subledger. Bank feeds/payment execution, external GL posting, invoice delivery, tax determination, currency conversion/FX revaluation, accounting periods, revenue-recognition policy, customer prepayments/overpayments/credit applications, and regulatory invoice qualification are additional integrations/policies. Each shipment carries one currency (USD by default); its invoices, bills, cash, credits and journal postings stay in that currency and summaries are grouped per currency without conversion. See [multi-currency](multi-currency.md). The included inclusive invoice reflects your agreed freight amount; it does not calculate jurisdiction-specific tax. Do not treat a local posting or export as confirmation of a remote accounting/payment action.

Charge approvals preserve the base agreement and freeze with the relevant side's posted billing. Actual post-approval carrier changes use the separate adjustment workflow; later customer additions use separate supplemental customer invoices. Cancelled shipments use [independent cancellation-fee obligations](cancellations.md) with explicit finance decisions, invoices/payables, retained paid-fee reversals/refunds, and the existing bank/credit-application ledger. Their original transport rates do not post. Consolidated multi-load invoice allocation and automatic accessorial pricing remain further work. Recorded gate duration does not establish a payable or customer charge.

Freight billing is separate from commerce product-order totals. The sync integration continues to write shipment tracking only; it does not overwrite an upstream order's sales, payment, or accounting state.
