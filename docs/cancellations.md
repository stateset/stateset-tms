# Cancellation fees

A cancelled shipment can still have a separately agreed truck-ordered-not-used charge or customer cancellation fee. Freight records that obligation without marking the freight delivered, fabricating POD, or posting the original transport buy/sell rates. [Carrier release](tenders.md) still establishes operational cancellation; a fee proposal never releases a carrier.

## Operational and finance workflow

1. Cancel an unassigned planned load, withdraw a proven-unsent offer, or request and record actual carrier-confirmed release. Picked-up freight cannot use this cancellation workflow. Resolve any late carrier custody/invoice incidents before fee decisions.
2. Open the cancelled shipment and select **Propose cancellation fee**. Choose customer charge or carrier cost, an explicit positive amount in the shipment's currency, description, and line-specific agreement reference. Attach an immutable shipment file or identify evidence held externally. Operations and finance can propose; a proposal posts no money.
3. A carrier cost must select the exact historical tender that was released with actual carrier confirmation. Its original carrier snapshot survives cancellation and subsequent changes to the directory. Multiple fee agreements can have independent targets, including fees involving different released carrier attempts; they do not restore carrier ownership. Customer agreements retain the proposing shipment customer's snapshot.
4. Finance opens **Freight audit → Cancellation fees** and selects **Review cancellation fee**. Verify the actual counterparty, agreement, and original file; record the contact, confirmation reference, and findings. Approval of a carrier cost requires the actual carrier invoice number and creates a prepared payable for that fee. Approval of a customer charge requires a new customer invoice number, due date at/after issuance, configured issuer, and an actual bill-to address in the proposal snapshot. It immediately issues that fee's frozen invoice. Rejection retains the proposal and decision without posting.
5. Download the customer cancellation invoice from the shipment or customer billing workspace. It identifies the shipment as cancelled, labels planned dates as planned, and itemizes only the agreed cancellation service. It omits carrier costs and internal contact/proof information. Recording issuance does not send the invoice.
6. Use **Bank reconciliation** for actual cleared receipts, payouts, and customer/carrier refunds. Each cancellation agreement has its own versioned target, so one fee cannot consume another's balance. Carrier payment can precede the cancelled shipment's planned pickup; the actual fee posting date is the earliest eligible date. Existing partial/batched allocation, counterparty, duplicate-bank-reference, reversal, and stale-version checks apply. Recording reconciliation does not send money.

The cancelled load's commercial totals use posted cancellation fees and active customer credits. Original buy/sell rates remain historical plan fields. Ordinary freight invoice approval and settlement still require delivery/POD; cancellation fees have their own agreement and journal source. Fee decisions do not generate EDI price/cancellation messages, commerce invoices, or tracking payloads containing finance data.

## Corrections and retained history

Finance can **Reverse cancellation fee** with actual counterparty confirmation, a reason/date, and current fee/load/target versions. The reversal appends opposite journal entries and preserves original invoices, amounts, proof, and bank allocations. Its effective date cannot precede posting or related bank/credit-application activity or be in the future.

- Reverse active customer credit notes before retracting a customer cancellation invoice. Preserve genuine receipts: retraction voids the invoice and exposes only net funds actually retained as a customer refund liability. Generic **Void invoice** is unavailable for fee invoices, because it would leave their agreement state inconsistent. Customer credits, including after payment with actual confirmation, remain available through normal billing; they reduce its receivable and commercial revenue. Reconcile an actual customer refund separately after the external return clears.
- Retracting an already paid carrier fee creates a negative balance on its original payable. Record an actual carrier refund, or a carrier-confirmed credit application to another eligible payable for that carrier. The original payout is retained. The application moves liability between its targets without posting expense or cash again.
- **Correct cancellation fee** creates a new proposal linked to the uncorrected rejected/reversed predecessor. It must retain shipment, side, counterparty, carrier attempt, and agreement reference. No implicit reuse or branching is allowed. Reapproval creates a new independent target; the old target and any credit/refund remain. A corrected carrier document may retain its actual invoice number only through that explicit reversed predecessor chain. Unrelated carrier invoices/adjustments and fee agreements cannot claim the same number for the same carrier. Customer invoice numbers remain unique, including void history.

Cleared customer funds are not automatically refunded or transferred to a replacement invoice. Reverse an erroneous reconciliation only when supported by the actual bank evidence. Use [customer refund reconciliation](customer-refunds.md) for actual returns; external refund execution and customer credit reassignment remain separate workflows; do not reverse a genuine receipt merely to force a correction through.

## Evidence, security, and integrations

Proposals retain creator/time and the original amount, counterparty snapshot, carrier tender, and evidence digest. Finance decisions and reversals record the authenticated subject. Stored evidence must belong to this tenant/load and cannot already be rejected. Fee approval verifies its exact bytes inside the business transaction. Cash posting against a cancellation target and carrier credit applications also verify supporting bytes. Corruption rolls back the entire mutation, including new targets, journals, audit, correction pointers, and idempotency receipts. A file supporting retained posted cancellation history cannot subsequently be rejected; its history and original bytes remain available.

`charges.write` permits proposals. `finance.write` permits decisions/reversals and existing bank/billing operations. Read access can inspect/download existing records; integration credentials cannot assert a human agreement. Body-supplied identities, amounts outside the supported positive range, wrong-load proof, unconfirmed/wrong carrier attempts, stale versions, and unexpected fields fail before mutation.

Late signed 210s against a released tender continue to enter the existing historical-carrier reconciliation queue. A 210 total does not establish cancellation eligibility or auto-approve a fee. Investigate and retain the original event/document, resolve the carrier incident with actual findings, then record the verified fee and invoice evidence. The existing gateway supports original 204 offers; partner cancellation/change messages remain commissioning work. No sibling repository is modified.

Cancellation persistence was introduced in [storage format 2](storage.md), added by migration `004_cancellation_fees.sql`. It upgrades format 1 atomically, retains prior migration checksums/history and existing business fields, and prevents a previous API from rewriting away cancellation records.

## API

- `POST /v1/loads/{id}/cancellation-fees`: `{expected_load_version, side: "buy"|"sell", tender_id?, amount_cents, description, evidence_reference, document_id?, corrects_id?}`. Carrier side requires its released tender; customer side must omit/null the tender.
- `POST /v1/cancellation-fees/{id}/review`: `{expected_load_version, expected_version, decision: "approve"|"reject", counterparty_contact, confirmation_reference, note, number?, due_date?}`. Approval requires an actual invoice number; only customer approval includes a due date.
- `POST /v1/cancellation-fees/{id}/reverse`: `{expected_load_version, expected_version, expected_target_version, reversed_on, counterparty_contact, confirmation_reference, note}`.

All mutations require `Idempotency-Key`. Workspace responses include `cancellation_fees` with retained decisions/correction links and existing `bills`/`settlements` with their normal balances/versions. Requesting the same successful command again replays its response without another target or posting.

Local qualification uses independently expected 25,001-cent carrier and 37,501-cent customer fees: actual payments leave zero balances and 12,500 cents of net cash movement. A retracted paid 25,001-cent fee followed by a corrected 15,001-cent fee reconciles to 15,001 cents of cost/net cash without changing the original payout/principal. A separate 10,000-cent cancelled payable consumes only 10,000 of an agreed 25,001-cent carrier credit, leaving 15,001 for an actual refund. Tests also inspect strict guards, erroneous receipt/credit correction and paid customer retraction retaining genuine receipts with a refund limited to actual funds received, invoice escaping, file corruption, competing PostgreSQL decisions/allocations, reconnect, role denial, production browser operation, and mobile controls. All records are synthetic; no live carrier agreement or bank transfer is represented as verified by these fixtures.
